To be classified as a berry, a fruit must meet three criteria:
*   It must derive from a single ovary of a single flower.
*   It must have a fleshy middle layer (mesocarp).
*   It must contain seeds (though modern commercial bananas have been bred to have tiny, infertile ovules instead of true seeds).

This creates a famous botanical irony: many things we call "berries" (like strawberries or raspberries) are not true berries, while bananas, watermelons, and even pumpkins are.

*   **Triploid:** Most commercial bananas are "triploid," meaning they have three sets of chromosomes instead of the usual two. This mutation makes them sterile, which is why they have no large seeds.
*   **Clonal:** Because they are sterile, every Cavendish banana is a genetic clone of the one before it. They are propagated by humans through "suckers" (clones taken from the base of the plant) rather than through seeds. This lack of genetic diversity is why the banana population is highly vulnerable to diseases like Panama Disease.

### 3. Structurally: It is an Accessory Fruit of a Herb
Technically, a banana plant is not a tree. Because the stem does not contain true woody tissue (lignin), the banana plant is classified as a **giant herbaceous plant**. 

The "trunk" you see is actually a **pseudostem**, made of tightly wrapped, overlapping leaf bases. Therefore, the fruit is the product of a herb, not a tree.

***

### Summary
If you want the most precise definition:
**A banana is a sterile, triploid, seedless berry produced by a large herbaceous plant.**

Multiply both sides by $(4 - \ln(3x))$ to remove the denominator:
$$14 = 423x(4 - \ln(3x))$$

Distribute $423x$:
$$14 = 1692x - 423x \ln(3x)$$

Rearrange to group terms with $x$:
$$423x \ln(3x) - 1692x = -14$$

Factor out $423x$:
$$423x (\ln(3x) - 4) = -14$$

Divide by $423$:
$$x(\ln(3x) - 4) = -\frac{14}{423}$$

### 3. Solve for $x$ using the Lambert W function
The equation is in the form $x(\ln(3x) + C) = D$. To use the Lambert W function (which solves equations of the form $we^w = z$), we need to manipulate the expression into an exponential form.

$$\frac{u}{3}(\ln(u) - 4) = -\frac{14}{423}$$
$$u(\ln(u) - 4) = -\frac{14}{141}$$

Substitute $u$ back into the equation:
$$(e^4 \cdot e^w) \cdot w = -\frac{14}{141}$$
$$w e^w = -\frac{14}{141 e^4}$$

This matches the form $w e^w = Y$. The solution for $w$ is:
$$w = W\left(-\frac{14}{141 e^4}\right)$$

Now, substitute $w$ back to find $u$:
$$\ln(u) - 4 = W\left(-\frac{14}{141 e^4}\right)$$
$$\ln(u) = 4 + W\left(-\frac{14}{141 e^4}\right)$$
$$u = e^{4 + W\left(-\frac{14}{141 e^4}\right)}$$

Since $u = 3x$, we have:
$$x = \frac{e^4 \cdot e^{W\left(-\frac{14}{141 e^4}\right)}}{3}$$

Using the identity $e^{W(z)} = \frac{z}{W(z)}$:
$$x = \frac{e^4}{3} \cdot \frac{-\frac{14}{141 e^4}}{W\left(-\frac{14}{141 e^4}\right)}$$
$$x = \frac{-14}{423 \cdot W\left(-\frac{14}{141 e^4}\right)}$$
