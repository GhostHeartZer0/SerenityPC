## Easy Baked Fudge Swirl Cheesecake

This is a simple homemade cheesecake with a chocolate fudge swirl baked right into it. It’s rich, creamy, and easy enough for a beginner.

### Ingredients

#### Crust
- 1½ cups graham cracker crumbs
- 6 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
- 2 tablespoons sugar

#### Cheesecake Filling
- 24 oz / 680 g cream cheese, softened to room temperature
- ¾ cup granulated sugar
- ⅓ cup sour cream
- 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- 1 small pinch of salt
- 3 large eggs, room temperature

#### Fudge Swirl
- 1 cup chocolate chips or chopped chocolate
- ¼ cup heavy cream
- 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
- 1 teaspoon vanilla extract, optional

#### Optional Fudge Sauce for Topping
- ½ cup chocolate chips
- 3 tablespoons heavy cream
- 1 teaspoon butter

---

### Instructions

#### 1. Make the crust
1. Preheat your oven to **350°F / 175°C**.
2. Mix the graham cracker crumbs, melted butter, and sugar until it looks like wet sand.
3. Press the mixture firmly into the bottom and up the sides of a **9-inch springform pan**.
4. Bake for **8–10 minutes**.
5. Remove from the oven and let cool while you make the filling.

#### 2. Make the cheesecake filling
1. In a large bowl, beat the softened cream cheese and sugar until smooth and creamy.
2. Mix in the sour cream, flour, vanilla, and salt.
3. Add the eggs one at a time, mixing just until combined.  
   **Do not overmix** — you want a smooth, creamy batter, not a fluffy one.

#### 3. Make the fudge swirl
1. In a small saucepan over low heat, warm the heavy cream and butter.
2. Remove from heat and stir in the chocolate chips until melted and smooth.
3. Stir in vanilla, if using.
4. Let it cool for a few minutes so it is warm but not hot.

#### 4. Assemble and bake
1. Pour the cheesecake filling into the prepared crust and smooth the top.
2. Drizzle or spoon the fudge mixture over the top in a few spots.
3. Use a knife or skewer to swirl through the batter for a marbled fudge effect.
4. Bake for **50–65 minutes**, until the edges are set but the center still jiggles a little when you gently shake the pan.

#### 5. Cool and chill
1. Let the cheesecake cool in the pan with the oven door cracked open for about **30 minutes**.
2. Remove the springform ring.
3. Refrigerate for at least **4 hours**, preferably overnight.

#### 6. Serve
Slice and serve chilled. For extra fudge, warm the optional fudge sauce and drizzle it over each slice.

---

### Tips for Success
- Let the cream cheese and eggs come to room temperature first — this helps prevent lumps and cracks.
- Do not overbeat the batter.
- A slightly wobbly center after baking is normal. It will set as it chills.
- If you want a stronger chocolate flavor, use semi-sweet chocolate chips for the swirl.

Git **merging** and **rebased** are two ways to combine changes from different branches, but they handle history differently.

## 1. What merging does

A merge combines two branches by creating a new **merge commit** that has two parents: the tip of the current branch and the tip of the branch being merged.

Example:

```text
A---B---C  main
        \
         D---E  feature
```

After `git merge feature` on `main`:

```text
A---B---C---M  main
        \     /
         D---E
```

The merge commit `M` records that `feature` was merged into `main`.

### Merge behavior

- Preserves the original commit history.
- Does not rewrite existing commits.
- Creates a merge commit unless the merge is a fast-forward.
- Conflicts are usually resolved once, for the whole merge.

---

## 2. What rebasing does

A rebase takes the commits from one branch and **replays them on top of another branch**.

Example:

```text
A---B---C  main
        \
         D---E  feature
```

If you run:

```bash
git checkout feature
git rebase main
```

Git replays `D` and `E` on top of `C`:

```text
A---B---C---D'---E'  feature
```

The new commits `D'` and `E'` are copies of `D` and `E`, but they have new commit IDs because their parent changed.

### Rebase behavior

- Rewrites commits.
- Produces a linear history.
- Can be used to reorder, squash, edit, or drop commits.
- Conflicts may need to be resolved commit by commit.

---

The main difference is that:

- **Merge preserves history** and adds a merge commit.
- **Rebase rewrites history** to make it look like the work was done directly on the base branch.
