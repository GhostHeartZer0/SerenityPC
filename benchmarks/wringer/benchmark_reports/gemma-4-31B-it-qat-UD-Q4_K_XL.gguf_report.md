Here is the breakdown of why:

If you turn off a flashlight, the light doesn't "stop"; the photons that were already traveling simply continue until they hit a surface. The "darkness" that follows is just the area where there are no longer any photons. Therefore, the darkness "arrives" at the same speed the light "leaves."

**The Exception (Geometric Shadows)**
In physics, there is a theoretical concept called a "shadow projection." If you cast a shadow on a very distant wall and move your hand quickly, the shadow on the wall can technically move **faster than the speed of light**. This does not violate the laws of physics because the shadow isn't a physical object transporting information or matter; it is just a geometric projection.

### 1. Botanically: It is a Berry
In botanical terms, a banana is classified as a **berry**. 

A true berry is a fleshy fruit produced from a single ovary of a single flower. Bananas fit all the scientific criteria:
*   They have a soft pericarp (the fleshy part).
*   They contain seeds (though in commercial bananas, these are tiny, infertile specks).
*   They develop from one flower with one ovary.

### 2. Structurally: It is an Herb
While we often call the banana "tree," it is not actually a tree. A tree has a woody trunk (secondary growth). A banana plant has a **pseudostem**—a stalk made of tightly packed leaf sheaths. 

Because it lacks true wood, the banana plant is technically a **perennial herbaceous plant** (a giant herb).

### 3. Genetically: It is a Clone
The bananas you buy at a grocery store (the **Cavendish** variety) are not "natural" in the way wild fruits are. They are **triploid hybrids**, meaning they have three sets of chromosomes.

Because they are sterile and seedless, they cannot be grown from seeds. Every Cavendish banana is a **genetic clone** of the original plant. They are propagated through "suckers" or tissue culture. This makes them dangerously susceptible to disease, as there is no genetic diversity to provide resistance.

### 4. The "True" Banana vs. The Plantain
In common language, we separate "bananas" (sweet) from "plantains" (starchy/cooking). However, botanically, **they are both bananas**. They belong to the genus *Musa*. The difference is simply in the starch-to-sugar ratio and how we use them in the kitchen.

### Summary
If you want to be scientifically precise: **A banana is a seedless berry produced by a giant herbaceous plant.**
