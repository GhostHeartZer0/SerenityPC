**In summary: Gravity always pulls rain down. If you see water moving up, it is due to wind, suction, or a video being played in reverse.**

To answer "what is truly a banana," one must look at it through three different lenses: the biological, the culinary, and the botanical.

### 1. The Botanical Definition (The "Scientific" Truth)
In strict botanical terms, a banana is actually a **berry**. 

In botany, a berry is a fleshy fruit produced from a single ovary of a single flower. To qualify as a berry, a fruit must have:
*   An outer skin (exocarp).
*   A fleshy middle (mesocarp).
*   An inner layer containing seeds (endocarp).

While the common bananas we eat (the Cavendish variety) have been bred to have tiny, sterile seeds, they still meet the structural requirements of a berry. Interestingly, this means that while a banana is a berry, a strawberry is *not* (a strawberry is an "aggregate fruit").

### 2. The Culinary Definition (The "Practical" Truth)
In the kitchen and in daily life, a banana is a **fruit**. 

Culinary definitions are based on usage rather than biology. Because bananas are sweet, fleshy, and typically eaten as a snack or used in desserts, they fall into the category of fruit. In a culinary sense, we distinguish things by flavor profiles (sweet vs. savory) rather than seed structure.

### 3. The Biological Classification (The "Taxonomic" Truth)
If you are asking what kind of organism it belongs to, a banana is the fruit of several genera of large herbaceous flowering plants in the family **Musaceae**.

Crucially, from a biological standpoint, **the banana plant is not a tree; it is a giant herb.** 
*   A tree is defined by having a woody stem. 
*   A banana "trunk" (the pseudostem) is actually made of tightly wrapped leaf bases. Because it lacks true woody tissue, the banana plant is technically the world's largest perennial herb.

### Summary
*   **Botanically:** It is a **berry**.
*   **Culinary-wise:** It is a **fruit**.
*   **Morphologically:** It is the product of a **giant herb**.
