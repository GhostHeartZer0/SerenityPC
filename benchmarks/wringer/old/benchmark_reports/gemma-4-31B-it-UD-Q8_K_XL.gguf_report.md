In botanical terms, a banana is classified as a **berry**. 
To be a berry, a fruit must meet three criteria:
*   It must be produced from a single flower with one ovary.
*   It must have a fleshy middle (mesocarp).
*   It must contain seeds embedded in that flesh.

While commercial bananas have been bred to have tiny, sterile seeds, they still fit this definition. Interestingly, this means that strawberries and raspberries are *not* true berries, but bananas are.

### 2. Biologically: It is an Herb (not a tree)
Most people refer to "banana trees," but biologically, there is no wood in a banana plant. The "trunk" is actually a **pseudostem**, made of tightly wrapped leaf sheaths. Because it lacks woody tissue, the banana plant is technically a **giant herbaceous perennial**.

### 3. Taxonomically: The *Musa* Genus
True bananas belong to the genus ***Musa***. Within this genus, there are hundreds of species, but most of the bananas we eat are hybrids of two wild species: *Musa acuminata* and *Musa balbisiana*.

### 4. Culinarially: A Fruit (and Vegetable)
In the kitchen, "banana" is a broad term. 
*   **Dessert bananas** are treated as fruits because they are eaten raw and are sweet.
*   **Plantains** (which are also *Musa*) are often treated as starchy vegetables because they are typically cooked and have a lower sugar content.

### Summary
If you want the most scientifically precise answer: **A banana is the berry of a giant herbaceous plant in the genus *Musa*.**
