### 1. Mist and Spray (Evaporation and Wind)
If you are near a crashing wave or a fountain, water droplets can be caught in a strong updraft or wind. This causes the water to travel upward, creating the illusion of "upward rain."

### 2. "Updrafts" in Storms
In severe thunderstorms, powerful currents of air called **updrafts** can push raindrops and hailstones back up into the sky. ### 3. Optical Illusions
Under certain lighting conditions (like driving through a heavy downpour at night with headlights on), the way light reflects off moving droplets can sometimes trick the human eye into perceiving movement in the wrong direction.

### 4. Capillary Action (Microscopic scale)
On a microscopic level, water can "climb" upward through very narrow spaces (like inside a plant stem or a paper towel) due to surface tension and adhesion. This is called **capillary action**, but it is not rain.

**Summary:** In nature, gravity always pulls liquid water downward. Any instance of water moving upward is caused by an opposing force, such as **wind, updrafts, or evaporation.**

Botanically speaking, a banana is a **berry**.

This sounds counterintuitive because we don't think of berries as long and yellow, but in botany, a berry is a fleshy fruit produced from a single ovary of a single flower. Bananas meet all the criteria:
*   They come from one ovary.
*   They have a fleshy middle (mesocarp).
*   They contain seeds (though the ones we eat have been bred to have tiny, non-functional seeds).

### 2. The Culinary Definition (The "Practical" Answer)
In the kitchen, a banana is a **fruit** used as a starch or a sweetener. Unlike most fruits that are treated as refreshing or acidic, bananas are categorized by their starch content:
*   **Plantains:** These are "cooking bananas." They are starchy, lower in sugar, and are treated more like a vegetable (like a potato) in cooking.
*   **Dessert Bananas:** These are the sweet, soft fruits we eat raw.

### 3. The Biological Reality (The "Genetic" Answer)
What you are likely eating right now is a specific cultivar called the **Cavendish banana**. 

Most modern bananas are **parthenocarpic**, meaning they develop fruit without fertilization. Because of this, the bananas we eat are clones of one another. They have no seeds, which means they cannot reproduce on their own. Every Cavendish banana in a grocery store is genetically identical to the one next to it.

### Summary
If you want to be technically precise: **A banana is a berry produced by a herbaceous perennial plant.**
